## Name

TAP / OUT

## Short description (Steam allows about 300 characters)

Ten people, one cold northern lake, a $400,000 prize. Build a shelter, keep a fire, fish, trap and
forage while autumn slides into a −20 °C winter. There is a sat phone in your pack. Press it, and it
is over. Last one out there wins.

## Long description

You are Dan Brandt, thirty-four, dropped on a lake shore in the far north with ten items, the
clothes on your back and a camera on a tripod. Nine other contestants are somewhere around the
lake. You will never see them, and nobody tells you when one goes home. The only way to know you
have won is the day the boat comes for you and not for them.

**Survive the slide into winter.** Over twenty to thirty days the leaves turn, the first frost
comes, the lake freezes and the cold goes down to −20 °C. Every hour costs food, water and warmth.
Chop wood, keep a fire, fish, trap, forage, smoke meat and get ready for the long dark.

**Build your camp step by step.** A tarp becomes a lean-to, then a framed hut, then a log cabin,
or dig an earth dugout instead. Every build goes up in small jobs you do by hand, over days. Add a
firepit, a smoking rack, a food cache, a bough bed and a stone stove. Late in the run Dan comes up
with new ideas: a reflector wall behind the fire, birch-bark shingles, a snow wall round the
shelter.

**Hunt by the sign.** Find tracks, follow the hoof prints through the woods, get close, draw the
bow and loose it yourself. Dan says what he sees as the hunt goes on.

**Dress for it.** A thick jacket and a rain shell go on and off in a minute. The jacket keeps the
cold out but makes you sweat when you work; the shell keeps the rain off. Carry the axe, saw,
shovel, knife or bow in your hands, and check your backpack any time.

**Respect the ice.** When winter comes the lake freezes, a little thicker every day. Chop a test
hole to see how thick it is, and walk out onto it if you dare. Thin ice slows the work and can drop
you into the lake. On storm days the shore rocks are slick.

**Walk it yourself.** A first-person camp, shore and woods, made of simple low-poly shapes. Day,
dusk, night, rain, storms, snow, northern lights and shooting stars, with loons, an eagle, foxes,
hares, grouse and ravens going about their lives around you.

**The hardest part is your head.** Hunger, cold and the quiet wear down your resolve. Dan thinks
out loud now and then, and talks to the show's camera, the only voice out there. He writes letters
he will not send. Thoughts of home creep in on the worst nights. The sat phone is always in your
pack, and if a night is going to kill you, the medics come and end your run for you.

**You decide.** No quest log, no daily goals, no one telling you what matters. The weather and
your body make the pressure; every choice is yours, including the odd choice the wild puts in
front of you.

- One run, one save: it saves as you play, and when the run ends, it is over.
- Runs of about 20 to 30 in-game days.
- Ambient sound and music made for the weather, with a quiet score for the big moments.
- Built on a carefully balanced survival simulation, tested over many simulated runs.

## AI disclosure (Steam's "Content Survey" wording)

AI tools were used in making this game. The code and the in-game text were written with the help
of an AI assistant (Claude) and reviewed by the developer. Dan's spoken voice is generated with
Kokoro-82M, an open-source text-to-speech model (Apache-2.0 licence). No AI generates anything
while you play.

## System requirements (Windows)

| | Minimum | Recommended |
|---|---|---|
| OS | Windows 10 64-bit | Windows 11 64-bit |
| Processor | 2 cores, 2 GHz | 4 cores |
| Memory | 4 GB RAM | 8 GB RAM |
| Graphics | Any graphics card from the last ten years (WebGL 2) | A separate graphics card |
| Storage | 500 MB | 500 MB |

The settings have a Lighter graphics option for older laptops, and the game offers to turn it on
if it stutters.
